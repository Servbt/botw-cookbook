Apple Cabbage Stew

Inspired by: Salt Pile, Red Apple, Cabbage
Real-world version: Nord-style hearth stew
Serves: 2–4 · Time: 20–60 min · Difficulty: Easy

Ingredients

  • Kosher salt
  • Red apple
  • Cabbage

Method

  1. Render a little bacon or butter in a heavy pot, then soften diced onion.
  2. Add shredded cabbage and cook until it wilts, 5 minutes.
  3. Stir in peeled diced apple, stock, and a pinch of salt; simmer 25 minutes.
  4. Finish with a knob of butter and plenty of black pepper.

Nordic home cooking at its plainest — sweet, sour, and deeply warming.

Beef Stew

Inspired by: Raw Beef, Carrot, Salt Pile, Garlic
Real-world version: Nord-style hearth stew
Serves: 2–4 · Time: 20–60 min · Difficulty: Easy

Ingredients

  • Raw beef
  • Carrot
  • Kosher salt
  • Garlic

Method

  1. Brown cubed beef hard in batches so it develops a crust, then set aside.
  2. Sweat onion, carrot, and garlic; splash in a little ale to deglaze.
  3. Return the beef with stock and a bay leaf; simmer covered for 90 minutes.
  4. Add potatoes for the last 30 minutes, then season and rest.

The longer it simmers, the better it is the next morning.

Cabbage Potato Soup

Inspired by: Potato, Salt Pile, Leek, Cabbage
Real-world version: simple tavern soup
Serves: 2–4 · Time: 20–60 min · Difficulty: Easy

Ingredients

  • Potato
  • Kosher salt
  • Leek
  • Cabbage

Method

  1. Cook leek and cabbage in butter until sweet and soft, 8 minutes.
  2. Add diced potato and stock, simmering until the potato falls apart.
  3. Mash a few potatoes against the pot to thicken, leaving the rest whole.
  4. Season, then swirl in a spoonful of cream if you like.

Cheap, filling, and better than its ingredients suggest.

Clam Chowder

Inspired by: Clam Meat, Potato, Jug of Milk, Butter
Real-world version: hearty crab or clam chowder
Serves: 2–4 · Time: 20–60 min · Difficulty: Easy

Ingredients

  • Clam meat
  • Potato
  • Whole milk
  • Butter
  • Kosher salt

Method

  1. Render bacon, then cook onion and celery in the fat until soft.
  2. Sprinkle in flour and cook 1 minute, then whisk in the clam liquor and milk.
  3. Add diced potato and simmer until tender, about 15 minutes.
  4. Fold in chopped clams and butter; warm gently without boiling.

A coastal tavern bowl; the clam liquor is the whole point.

Ingredients

  • Kosher salt
  • Angelfish
  • Butter or oil

Method

  1. Pat the fillets dry and salt them 10 minutes ahead.

Best when the skin is crisp enough to crackle.

Elsweyr Fondue

Inspired by: Eidar Cheese Wheel, Moon Sugar, Ale
Real-world version: ale-cheese fondue
Serves: 2–4 · Time: 20–60 min · Difficulty: Easy

Ingredients

  • Aged cheddar, gouda, or gruyère
  • Brown sugar or honey
  • Amber ale, beer, or nonalcoholic malt drink
  • Kosher salt

Method

  1. Warm ale gently in a heavy pot — never let it boil.
  2. Add the cheese by handfuls, stirring until each melts before the next.
  3. Sweeten with a spoon of brown sugar or honey to round the sharpness.
  4. Serve hot with bread, apples, and roasted vegetables for dipping.

A pot of cheese and ale for sharing on a long winter night.

  3. Crack the legs and toss in the butter.
  4. Serve hot with extra butter for dipping.

The most direct route from crab to butter to you.

Tomato Soup

Inspired by: Tomato, Salt Pile, Garlic, Leek
Real-world version: simple tavern soup
Serves: 2–4 · Time: 20–60 min · Difficulty: Easy

Ingredients

  • Tomato
  • Kosher salt
  • Garlic
  • Leek

Method

  1. Soften garlic and leek in butter without colouring.
  2. Add chopped tomato and stock; simmer 20 minutes.
  3. Blend smooth and season, adding a pinch of sugar if sharp.
  4. Finish with a swirl of cream or a knob of butter.

A tavern staple; a pinch of sugar balances the acid.

Vegetable Soup

Inspired by: Cabbage, Potato, Leek, Tomato
Real-world version: simple tavern soup
Serves: 2–4 · Time: 20–60 min · Difficulty: Easy

Ingredients

  • Cabbage
  • Potato
  • Leek
  • Tomato
  • Kosher salt

Method

  1. Sweat cabbage, leek, and potato in butter until glossy.
  2. Add tomato and enough stock to cover; simmer until tender.
  3. Season well and let it bubble a little longer for depth.
  4. Serve chunky and hot with crusty bread.

Whatever the garden gave you, simmered into a bowl.

Venison Chop

Inspired by: Salt Pile, Venison
Real-world version: seared game cut
Serves: 2–4 · Time: 20–60 min · Difficulty: Easy

Ingredients

  • Kosher salt
  • Venison, beef, or lamb
  • Butter or oil

Method

  1. Bring the chop to room temperature and season boldly.
  2. Sear in a hot pan, basting with butter and thyme.
  3. Cook to medium-rare, then rest on a warm plate.
  4. Slice and spoon over the rested pan juices.

Serve it pink; overcooking venison is a small tragedy.

Venison Stew

Inspired by: Venison, Salt Pile, Potato, Leek
Real-world version: Nord-style hearth stew
Serves: 2–4 · Time: 20–60 min · Difficulty: Easy

Ingredients

  • Venison, beef, or lamb
  • Kosher salt
  • Potato
  • Leek

Method

  1. Brown the venison hard, then set aside.
  2. Sweat leek and potato, then deglaze with a little ale.
  3. Return the meat with stock and simmer until tender.
  4. Season deeply and serve with a hunk of bread.

The stew that tastes like coming in from the snow.
